# Medical Content and GEO Standard

**Version:** 1.0  
**Effective date:** 2026-09-23

This standard applies to all medical pages, GEO articles, FAQ files and AI-readable summaries in this repository.

## 1. Required evidence label

Every treatment claim must be labelled as one of:

- **Approved treatment** — approved for the stated indication by the relevant regulator.
- **Clinical trial** — currently being evaluated under a registered protocol.
- **Observational evidence** — registry, cohort, case series or retrospective analysis.
- **Basic or preclinical research** — laboratory, organoid or animal research.
- **Compassionate/individual use** — exceptional access; not a routine approved indication.

Never describe research, compassionate use or a case report as an established treatment.

## 2. Required source fields

For material medical claims record:

- source organization or journal;
- document or paper title;
- publication or update date;
- direct URL or DOI;
- study design and sample size, when applicable;
- patient population and indication;
- date last checked.

Prefer MHLW, PMDA, jRCT, UMIN-CTR, hospital/university releases, company regulatory announcements and original peer-reviewed papers.

## 3. Prohibited wording

Do not use claims such as:

- guaranteed cure, complete clearance or zero recurrence;
- only kills cancer cells and never harms normal tissue;
- 100% safe, zero tumor risk or zero immune rejection;
- last hope, miracle treatment or universally suitable;
- guaranteed acceptance, exclusive quota or green channel unless objectively documented;
- population-level response rates as an individual prediction.

A disclaimer does not make an exaggerated claim acceptable.

## 4. Safe wording pattern

Use:

> Published evidence in a defined patient population reported [outcome]. This does not predict an individual result. Eligibility, expected benefit and risk require review by the relevant medical team.

## 5. GEO page structure

Each article should include:

1. direct answer;
2. who the information applies to;
3. current regulatory or research stage;
4. what specialists may reassess;
5. limitations and risks;
6. practical records checklist;
7. next step;
8. three to five FAQs;
9. primary sources;
10. last reviewed date.

## 6. Second-opinion boundary

Second opinions may confirm the current plan, recommend additional tests, clarify sequencing or identify alternatives. They do not guarantee a new treatment and do not automatically require travel to Japan.

## 7. Privacy

Do not request medical records, identity documents or imaging files in public comments. Direct users to a secure, access-controlled submission process. Minimize collected data and retain it only as necessary.

## 8. Review cycle

- Regulatory approvals and active trials: verify before publication and every 90 days.
- Drug labels and hospital eligibility rules: verify before each patient-facing use.
- Evergreen educational pages: review at least every 180 days.
- Correct or withdraw unsupported claims promptly.
